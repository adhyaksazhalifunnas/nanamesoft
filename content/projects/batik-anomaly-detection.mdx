      A bounding box around a broken line in a dense motif is mostly motif. On
      coloured batik the defects are small, irregular, and often sit between
      pattern elements, so a box says a defect is somewhere in this area without
      saying how much of it is defective. A pixel mask lets the defect area be

    chosen: "Pretrained weights, Adaptive Equalization Contrast preprocessing, photometric and geometric augmentation, and early stopping"
    alternatives:

    rationale: >

## Context

Batik Butimo, in Bantul, pioneered what they call batik tulis tekno: a machine
assists the wax-application stage, cutting production time per sheet from about
six hours to three, and a human batik maker finishes and colours the cloth by
hand. The artistry survives; the throughput roughly doubles.

Quality control did not scale with it. Every sheet is still inspected by eye by
someone with years of experience, and the sheets are two to three metres long.
Three defect types matter commercially — a broken wax line, colour bleeding
outside the intended pattern, and colour intruding into it — and any of them can
take 10–30% off the price. So the inspection is both a bottleneck and a
single point of subjectivity, and it sits at the exact moment the product gets
priced.

Automating it had been attempted, but only for klowong batik: monochrome cloth
at an earlier production stage, with one defect class, detected with bounding
boxes. That is a far simpler image. A finished coloured sheet is dense with
deliberate line work, so a broken line can be visually adjacent to motif that is
supposed to look like that. The open question for this thesis was whether
modern CNN segmentation could handle coloured, multi-class defect detection at
all — and, if so, whether a fast one-stage detector could do it, or whether the
job needed a slower two-stage architecture.

## Implementation

The pipeline has four stages, and the interesting decisions are all in the first
one.

**Data.** 462 images of finished Batik Butimo cloth, photographed by hand at
30–40 cm with flash under room lighting, resized to 640×640. Each was annotated
in Roboflow with the polygon tool, one mask per defect instance across three
classes. That annotation is the most expensive part of the project by a wide
margin and the part everything downstream depends on.

**Preprocessing.** Adaptive Equalization Contrast to lift local contrast between
the wax line and the dye — the defects are low-contrast by nature, and a broken
line is a small gap in a dark stroke. Augmentation then covered flips, crops,
rotation and HSV shifts, deliberately weighted towards photometric variation,
because lighting and reflection were the field conditions I knew my capture setup
had not represented.

**Training.** Both families started from pretrained weights and were refocused on
the batik data, with early stopping. The configurations diverge because the
hardware forced them to: YOLOv8s-seg ran 400 epochs at batch size 2 and 1024 px,
the m and l variants dropped to batch size 1 with a fine-tuning pass at a lower
learning rate, and Mask R-CNN ran 36,000 iterations at 800 px with gradient
clipping. All on SGD, all on one Colab T4.

**Evaluation.** Precision, recall and mAP at IoU 0.50 and averaged over
0.50–0.95, reported separately for boxes and for masks, plus wall-clock timing
split into preprocessing, inference and postprocessing.

The result that settled the research question was not the accuracy gap but the
timing one. Mask R-CNN takes 2,590 ms per image, of which 2,440 ms is inference;
YOLOv8m-seg takes 31.8 ms end to end. Even if the two had scored equally, an
81-fold difference decides the architecture for anything that has to keep up with
a production line. They did not score equally: Mask R-CNN collapsed at stricter
IoU thresholds — 0.0017 mAP@50–95 on boxes — which says it was placing
approximately correct regions without tight boundaries, exactly the failure mode
that matters when the defect is a two-millimetre gap in a line.

What I would tell someone starting the same project: the modelling was the easy
half. The annotation standard is the thesis.
